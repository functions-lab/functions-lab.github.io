01

Accelerated & Software-Defined Wireless Systems

NextG networks must process Gbps-scale wireless signals with sub-millisecond deadlines. We build real-time PHY and vRAN systems on GPUs, heterogeneous accelerators, and RFSoC-based software-defined radios, from LDPC decoding and real-time Zak-OTFS receivers to elastic GPU sharing and multi-channel beamforming, and run them over the air on real testbeds.

02

Edge & Physical AI

Robots, drones, and vehicles need to perceive the physical world reliably, even where cameras and LiDAR fail. We turn low-cost radars and cellular base stations into sensors, with radar point clouds and odometry for ground and aerial vehicles and integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) on 5G infrastructure, and we study how to keep these sensors trustworthy against physical-layer attacks. We also build systems that make AI inference efficient on resource-constrained edge devices.

03

Spectrum Intelligence & RF Digital Twins

Sharing spectrum safely requires knowing where signals go and who is using them. We build scalable, physically accurate RF digital twins from real-world 3D scenes and measurements, and real-time spectrum monitoring and zone-management systems that let commercial networks coexist with incumbents, from radar and satellites to radio astronomy.

04

Analog & In-Physics Computing

Running ML on resource-constrained edge devices demands memory and compute they don't have. We compute directly in the physics of RF and analog signals, using waveform design, common electronics, and wireless broadcast of model weights to enable ultra-low-power inference for edge and physical AI. Our Science Advances paper showed a single passive RF mixer performing large-scale fully connected layers.

05

Agentic AI for Networked & Embedded Systems

AI agents can now write code, but networked and embedded systems are bound to real hardware, timing, and physics. We build agents and tools that design, deploy, operate, and debug these systems: generating wireless DSP and systems on RFSoCs, controlling optical networks and photonic instruments, and developing embedded and IoT firmware.

06

Intelligent Optical & Quantum Networks

Optical networks carry nearly all Internet and telecom traffic, yet are still provisioned conservatively and largely by hand. We build ML models of optical components, optical network digital twins, and automated provisioning and monitoring methods, validated on deployed testbed and metro networks. We also turn deployed fiber into a platform for sensing and quantum networking, with field trials showing quantum links and fiber sensing coexisting with coherent 400 GbE and 5G services.

07

Platforms & Testbeds

Our work runs on real hardware at scale: the city-scale NSF PAWR COSMOS platform in West Harlem, NYC, a Duke-campus wireless-optical testbed, and software-defined radios (USRP, Xilinx RFSoC) with sub-6 GHz and mmWave (28/60/140 GHz) front ends, edge servers with CPUs/GPUs/ASICs, and coherent optical transceivers.
